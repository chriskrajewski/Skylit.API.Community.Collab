# Gamma (Γ) & GEX (Gamma Exposure)

> The Greek that creates the most observable dealer flow. The curvature of options pricing. The reason markets pin or accelerate.

## Definition
**What it measures:** The rate of change of delta per $1 move in the underlying. The "second derivative" of option price with respect to spot.
**Why it matters:** If delta tells you where you're going, gamma tells you how fast you're getting somewhere new. High gamma = delta moves a lot per dollar of underlying movement. Low gamma = delta stays stable.
**Sign convention:**
- **Long options** ALWAYS have positive gamma (calls AND puts)
- **Short options** ALWAYS have negative gamma (calls AND puts)
- Sign is determined by POSITION (long/short), not option TYPE (call/put)

## Gamma's defining properties
### Gamma is highest at-the-money (ATM)
At ATM, there's maximum uncertainty about where the option will land at expiration. Small price moves can flip the option from OTM to ITM or vice versa. That uncertainty means delta shifts rapidly — high gamma.
### Gamma explodes near expiration
As expiration approaches, time value collapses and small price moves have outsized impact on whether the option finishes in or out of the money. 0DTE ATM options have extreme gamma in the final hours.
### Gamma is symmetric
For the same strike and expiration, calls and puts have the same gamma (under the same conditions). Sign depends on whether you're long or short.
### Gamma scales with IV
- Short-dated + low IV = **explosive gamma**
- Long-dated + high IV = very little gamma
- 0DTE options have the most extreme gamma at ATM

## Gamma by DTE — the table
| DTE | Gamma Behavior | Dealer Hedging Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **0 DTE** | Massive at ATM, near-zero elsewhere | Constant — sometimes minute by minute |
| **1–5 DTE** | High at ATM, falling off rapidly OTM | Frequent |
| **7–30 DTE** | Moderate, broader distribution | Regular |
| **30+ DTE** | Low, broad, flat | Periodic |

## GEX — Gamma Exposure
**Gamma Exposure (GEX)** is the aggregate net of dealer gamma across all open options positions at a given strike. It's one of the most important structural signals in modern options markets.
The sign of aggregate dealer GEX defines the entire market regime.
- **Positive GEX (+GEX):** Dealers are net long gamma. Their hedges are **contrarian** to price:
  - Price Rises -> Sells stock -> Counteracts rally
  - Price Falls -> Buys stock -> Cushions decline
  - **Result:** Market stabilizes around major strikes. Pinning behavior. Mean reversion.
  - **Visual on Heatseeker:** Yellow "Pika nodes"
- **Negative GEX (-GEX):** Dealers are net short gamma. Their hedges are **pro-cyclical** with price:
  - Price Rises -> Buys stock -> Amplifies rally
  - Price Falls -> Sells stock -> Amplifies decline
  - **Result:** Market accelerates moves. Trending behavior. Breakouts real.
  - **Visual on Heatseeker:** Purple "Barney nodes"
