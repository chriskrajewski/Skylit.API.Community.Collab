## 1. Concrete Setups & Patterns

### The "Whipsaw" Range Trade

- **Setup:** Price is trapped between two equal-strength magnets (e.g., a Negative GEX Node at 6485 and a Negative GEX Node at 6500) [01:21:28].
- **Logic:** When two opposing nodes have equal "pull" (e.g., -75M vs -80M), price will ping-pong between them.
- **Execution:** Short the upper node (6500) and long the lower node (6485).
- **Exit:** Sell into the opposing node. Do not hold for a breakout.

### The "First & Second Bounce" Rule

- **Rule:** A node is reliable for a bounce/rejection on the 1st and 2nd attempt.
- **Probability:** The probability of holding is ~66% for the first two tests.
- **Fade:** Avoid trading the 3rd or 4th test, as the probability of breaking through increases significantly [01:10:22].

### The "V-Shape" Liquidity Hunt

- **Setup:** Price breaks a major purple node (e.g., 6500) but hunts liquidity 3-5 points below it (undercut).
- **Logic:** If the node's value (magnet strength) increases during the drop, the break is a "fakeout" or liquidity hunt.
- **Execution:** Enter calls on the V-shape reversal back up through the node [01:18:25].

## 2. New or Refined Concepts

### Absolute Value Rule

- **Concept:** The sign (positive/negative) matters less than the Absolute Value of the node. A -80M node is a stronger magnet/wall than a +20M node [01:41:00].
- **Yellow vs. Purple:**
  - **Yellow (Positive GEX):** Suppressive. Price slows down and softens as it approaches.
  - **Purple (Negative GEX):** Oppressive/Volatile. "Fuel on fire." Price moves aggressively into these nodes [46:25].

### Node "Rate of Change" (Velocity)

- **Signal:** If price is stagnant but a specific node grows by 50% (e.g., 10M to 15M), that node is becoming a dominant magnet.
- **Application:** If a lower node grows while price is flat, dealers are positioning for a drop. Expect price to gravitate there [01:12:41].

### "Price Delivery" Context

- **Concept:** Always check where price came from. If price was delivered from a King Node (e.g., rejected 51 to hit 44), do not blindly bet on a return to 51. The rejection has already occurred [01:50:18].

## 3. Operational Workflow Items

### The "First 15 Minutes" Shuffle

- **Workflow:** Do not size up between 9:30 AM – 10:00 AM ET. This period sees the most opening/closing of positions, causing nodes to shift rapidly.
- **Stability:** Wait until 10:15 AM ET for the map to stabilize before taking high-conviction trades [52:43].

### Limit Order Execution Only

- **Rule:** Never use market orders. Always "Lowball the Bid."
- **Technique:** Place limit buys for calls on red candles (dips) into GEX floors. Place limit sells for calls on green candles (pumps) into GEX ceilings [01:44:42].
- **Why:** Captures the spread and avoids buying inflated premiums during FOMO spikes.

### Trinity Alignment Check

- **Workflow:** SPX, SPY, and QQQ must tell the same story.
- **Divergence:** If SPX shows upside but QQQ shows massive downside, sit out.
- **Confluence:** If SPX is near a call wall (resistance) AND QQQ shows a massive downside magnet, enter SHORT [01:24:47].

## 4. Rules & Edge Cases

### The "Plus/Minus 5" Rule

- **Rule:** Major nodes (Magnets/Walls) have a variance of ±5 points on SPX.
- **Application:** Do not set a hard stop exactly at the level. Allow for a 5-point overshoot (liquidity hunt) before the reversal triggers [47:32].

### Size = Stop Loss

- **Risk Management:** Do not use hard stops on 0DTE options because volatility will wick you out. Instead, size the position small enough that you can withstand a 100% loss ("Size for Zero") but aim for 3:1 R/R [57:46].

### The "Breakout" Trap

- **Signal:** If price breaks above a resistance node (e.g., 6500) but the node's negative gamma increases (e.g., -75M to -83M), the breakout is fake. The magnet is pulling it back down.
- **Action:** Take profits on calls or enter shorts on the pop [01:15:06].
