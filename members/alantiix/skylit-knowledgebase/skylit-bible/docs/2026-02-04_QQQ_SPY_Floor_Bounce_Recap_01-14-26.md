# QQQ/SPY Floor Bounce Recap: Jan 14, 2026 Analysis

**Source:** [YouTube - QQQ/SPY Floor Bounce Recap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2u_N4TajTmg)

This analysis covers a specific trade recap by Nico (associated with the Heatseeker community), focusing on a "Floor Bounce" setup where Trinity alignment was imperfect but actionable due to lack of downside volume on key tickers.

---

## 1. Concrete Setups & Patterns

### The "Empty Basement" Setup

- **Configuration:** SPY and QQQ hit major floor nodes (SPY 687, QQQ 616) with zero significant volume/nodes underneath them [01:04].
- **Logic:** Even if SPX shows a lower magnet (e.g., 6850), the lack of "fuel" (Gamma/Volume) below SPY and QQQ suggests they will act as a "hard floor" and prevent SPX from reaching its lower target.
- **Outcome:** SPY/QQQ held the floor, forcing SPX to respect the upper part of its structure (6900/6890).

### The "Retest Entry" (Missed Bottom Adaptation)

- **Scenario:** The initial "pure bottom" touch at 615.88 was missed.
- **Execution:** Instead of chasing the $1.00 pump, the trader waited for the retrace back to the level (616) to enter [02:22].
- **Criteria:** Entry taken at the start of the strong candle following the retrace.

## 2. New or Refined Concepts

### "2 out of 3" Trinity Filtering

- **Concept:** SPX had a bearish magnet (6850), but SPY and QQQ were at hard floors with no downside volume.
- **Decision:** The trader biased towards the 2 (SPY/QQQ) and treated the SPX bearish node as "low likelihood" because the other two indices physically had no room to drop [01:12].

### Structural Support Confluence

- **Refinement:** The Heatseeker node (687 on SPY) was cross-referenced with a chart structural level (a high wick from Dec 22/23 that acted as resistance-turned-support) [01:51]. This "Heatseeker + Chart History" confluence increased conviction.

## 4. Rules & Edge Cases

### Divergence Acceptance

- **Edge Case:** SPY was significantly weaker than QQQ on this day.
- **Rule:** Do not let divergence paralyze the trade. If the "Floor" logic holds on the strongest ticker (QQQ), the trade is valid, even if the weaker ticker (SPY) lags or creates a "nervous" chart [02:54].

### Spread vs. Naked Calls

- **Strategy:** For the index with the "scary" magnet (SPX 6850), the trader used a Credit Spread (defined risk, thesis: "won't drop below X") rather than naked calls, allowing him to hold through the chop while the magnet decayed [03:20].
